# Utility-scale lithium-ion battery CAPEX (about 4 hours)

Scope of these notes: lithium-ion, about 2–6 hours, with pack-only prices kept separate from system or installed CAPEX. Geography is Europe first; China and the United States are bounds. Years covered are published 2022–2026 figures plus 2030 (and nearby) forecasts. The model baseline to compare against, not re-derived here, is BloombergNEF country costs already in the model: Europe high-income peer (Germany/UK mean) about $1,011/kW in 2025 (~$253/kWh at 4 h) and about $727/kW in 2030 (~$182/kWh), nominal USD. China in that same table is about $477/kW in 2025 and about $360/kW in 2030.

Dollar years, duration, and scope are quoted as published. Where a $/kWh figure is arithmetic on a published total (for example Lazard’s project dollars divided by 400 MWh), it is marked as derived.

## What 2024–2026 installed system CAPEX do public sources report?

### Takeaway
For a ~4-hour lithium-ion system in 2024–2025, published **global** fully installed or all-in costs cluster around **$125–192/kWh**, while **Europe turnkey system prices** in the BloombergNEF survey are higher, at **$177/kWh in 2025** after **about $275/kWh in 2024**. US fully installed overnight costs are higher still (**NREL $334/kWh in 2024 dollars; Lazard about $155–400/kWh**). China’s 2025 turnkey average is **$73/kWh**. These are not the same scope: pack, turnkey equipment, and fully installed AC project cost must not be averaged together.

### Cited Findings

**BloombergNEF pack survey (pack only, not system CAPEX)** — [BNEF, 9 Dec 2025](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-transport/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-fall-to-108-per-kilowatt-hour-despite-rising-metal-prices-bloombergnef/); [BNEF, record lows](https://about.bnef.com/insights/clean-transport/new-record-lows-for-battery-prices/)

- Volume-weighted average lithium-ion **pack** price across end uses fell 8% from 2024 to a record **$108/kWh in 2025**. Cell prices fell 5% to **$74/kWh**. Packs are described as 93% below 2010 in real terms (about $1,474/kWh in real 2025 dollars, per Energy-Storage.News’ reading of the same survey).
- **Stationary-storage packs** were **$70/kWh in 2025**, 45% below 2024, and the cheapest segment. Lowest observed LFP stationary prices were **$36/kWh cell** and **$50/kWh pack**.
- Battery-electric-vehicle packs were **$99/kWh**.

**BloombergNEF Energy Storage Systems Cost Survey (turnkey system, not pack)** — [Energy-Storage.News, 16 Dec 2025](https://www.energy-storage.news/battery-storage-system-prices-continue-to-fall-sharply-bnef-and-ember-reports-find/), citing the 10 Dec 2025 survey (paywalled; no BNEF press release with the system table was retrieved)

- 2025 global average **turnkey** BESS price: **$117/kWh**, 31% below a restated 2024 figure of **$169/kWh**. BNEF inflation-adjusted the prior headline (last year’s survey had said **$165/kWh**). Prices are the lowest since the survey began in 2017. The 2023-to-2024 drop was about 40%.
- Duration split, global averages, 2025: **2-hour $124/kWh**; **4-hour $110/kWh**. Sample: 596 unique submissions.
- 2025 regional turnkey averages (duration mix not restated in the article): **China $73/kWh**, **Europe $177/kWh**, **United States $219/kWh**.
- Europe’s year-on-year system-cost decline was **37%**, steeper than China’s **29%**, attributed to Chinese overcapacity reaching Europe. Implied 2024 Europe turnkey is about $177/0.63 ≈ **$281/kWh** (derived). An earlier Energy-Storage.News account of the **2024** survey reported turnkey averages of **China $101/kWh**, **US $236/kWh**, **Europe $275/kWh** (1-, 2- and 4-hour systems grouped for China) — [Energy-Storage.News on the pack survey, referencing the prior system survey](https://www.energy-storage.news/li-ion-battery-pack-prices-fell-8-since-last-year-despite-metals-prices-rising-bloombergnef-says/). The inflation restatement and the 37% Europe decline are consistent with that 2024 Europe level within rounding.
- Scope caveat: IRENA, citing BNEF, defines “turnkey” energy-storage costs as **battery provider and system-integrator prices, excluding EPC, grid connection, and development** (see IRENA 2023 below). The 2025 Energy-Storage.News piece also says “turnkey” and compares to the prior survey, so the **$110/kWh 4-hour and $177/kWh Europe figures should not be treated as fully installed AC project CAPEX** unless a primary BNEF footnote says otherwise. That footnote was not in the public article.

**Ember, all-in project CAPEX (not pack)** — same Energy-Storage.News article, citing Ember, *How Cheap is Battery Storage?* (Ember’s own PDF was not retrieved; figures below are as reported by Energy-Storage.News on 16 Dec 2025)

- As of **October 2025**, all-inclusive BESS capex for a **4-hour or longer** project, averaged across markets **excluding China and the United States**, was **$125/kWh**.
- Split: about **$75/kWh** core equipment shipped from China (enclosure, power conversion system, energy management system) and about **$50/kWh** installation and connection.
- Grid-connection fees in the underlying cases ranged from **$30/kWh to $100/kWh**. Core equipment can be **$100/kWh or more** higher where tariffs, standards, or local-content rules apply.
- Shorter-duration systems were estimated **10–15%** more expensive because some components scale with MW rather than MWh.
- Evidence base stated by Ember via this article: recent auctions in **Italy, India, and Saudi Arabia**, plus expert interviews. Levelised cost of storage in that work: **$65/MWh** (LCOS, not CAPEX).
- Italy is inside the average, but the **$125/kWh is not a Germany/UK figure**.

**IRENA, installed project cost** — [Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2024 (digital report)](https://www.irena.org/Digital-Report/Renewable-Power-Generation-Costs-in-2024); [Renewable power generation costs in 2023](https://www.irena.org/Publications/2024/Sep/Renewable-Power-Generation-Costs-in-2023) (text read from a PDF mirror of the 2023 report)

- **2024:** total installed cost of **utility-scale** battery energy storage systems **$192/kWh**, down 93% from **$2,571/kWh in 2010**. Duration is not stated in the extracted 2024 text. Chemistry note: LFP dominates utility deployment. The 2024 text does not separately label the dollar year; the 2023 report uses **2023 USD**.
- **2023:** battery storage **project** costs **$273/kWh** (2023 USD), down 89% from **$2,511/kWh in 2010**. This is a global weighted average and is pulled by China (almost half of 2023 GWh additions).
- **2023 turnkey** (BNEF, as described by IRENA): global weighted average **between $325/kWh and $260/kWh by duration**. Moving from **1 hour to 4 hours** lowers the price by **around 20%**. These turnkey figures **exclude EPC, grid connection, and development**.
- **2023 cells** (BNEF via IRENA): lithium-ion cells **$139/kWh** (−14% vs 2022); LFP cells **$95/kWh**, 32% below nickel-manganese-cobalt cells. LFP was 84% of 2023 GWh additions (33% in 2020).
- Regional 2023 turnkey movement (percentages, not a full price table; the chart OCR did not yield clean regional $/kWh): China −36% (4-hour) to −29% (2-hour); Europe the smallest decline, −16% (1-hour) to −4% (4-hour), but still below US prices. US cost ratio vs global for a **1-hour** system in 2023: **1.61**.
- **Behind-the-meter residential, Germany:** **$892/kWh in 2022** and **$862/kWh in Q3 2023** (small-scale residential, not utility).

**NREL / NLR Annual Technology Baseline, US utility, fully installed overnight** — Cole, Ramasamy, and Olmez Turan, *Cost Projections for Utility-Scale Battery Storage: 2025 Update*, 27 June 2025, [PDF](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy25osti/93281.pdf), DOI 10.2172/2583471; [ATB 2025 utility-scale battery page](https://atb.nlr.gov/electricity/2025/utility-scale_battery_storage)

- Reference plant: **60 MW**, durations 2–10 h, **LFP**. Base-year costs are a bottom-up model for **Q1 2024 US** installations (PV System Cost Model, modeled market price).
- **2024 overnight capital cost, 4-hour, complete system: $334/kWh in 2024 dollars** (= **$1,336/kW**, because the report states $/kW = $/kWh × duration).
- Near-term 4-hour overnight costs, still **2024$/kWh**: **2025** low/mid/high **$295 / $321 / $350**; **2026** **$255 / $308 / $366**. One 2024 cost is used for all three trajectories; the spread opens after 2024.
- Component stack for the **2024** 4-hour system (Figure 1): **lithium-ion battery cabinets $210/kWh** of the **$334/kWh** total. Cabinets include LFP modules, racks, fire suppression, thermal management, and battery management, delivered to the installer. They are not cell-only prices.
- Fitted split: **energy cost $241/kWh** plus **power cost $372/kW** (4 h × 241 + 372 = $1,336/kW).
- Scope: owner’s **overnight** capital cost. Included: cabinets, bidirectional inverter, structural BOS, electrical BOS (transformer, switchgear, conductors, SCADA, MV/HV substation equipment, transmission line, and network-upgrade **equipment**), installation labor, permitting and interconnection **studies**, sales tax at a 5.8% national average, contingency, developer overhead, and 5% profit on hardware. **Interconnection upgrade costs are excluded.** Land rent is not listed as a line. Values were converted to 2024$ with the CPI when taken from other publications.
- The ATB also updates **commercial and residential** storage with the same 2025 methods. Those $/kWh were not extracted here.

**Lazard LCOE+ , June 2025, US, unsubsidized capital-cost assumptions** — [Lazard LCOE+ June 2025 PDF](https://www.lazard.com/media/5tlbhyla/lazards-lcoeplus-june-2025-_vf.pdf)

- Use case: **utility-scale standalone, 100 MW, 4-hour, 400 MWh usable**, 90% depth of discharge, 10% overbuild, 20-year life. Dollar year is not labeled; the report is dated June 2025.
- **Initial capital cost, DC: $107–232/kWh.** **AC: $25–67/kW** (about **$6–17/kWh** at 4 h; derived). **EPC: $28–116/kWh.**
- **Total initial installed cost: $62–160 million** for that 100 MW / 400 MWh plant. Derived unit cost: **$155–400/kWh**, or **$620–1,600/kW**. Lazard’s note: initial installed cost includes inverter, module, balance-of-system, and EPC. Whether grid-upgrade costs sit inside EPC is not stated.
- Unsubsidized **LCOS** (not CAPEX): **$115–254/MWh** for the 100 MW, 4-hour case. With the storage ITC, the summary chart shows about **$83–192/MWh**. The 2024 edition’s unsubsidized 100 MW / 400 MWh LCOS was **$170–296/MWh** (Energy-Storage.News).
- **C&I standalone is a different product:** 1 MW, **2-hour** (not 4-hour). DC capital **$238–445/kWh**, EPC **$56–168/kWh**, unsubsidized LCOS **$319–506/MWh**.

**IEA, *Batteries and Secure Energy Transitions*, 2024** — [executive summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/batteries-and-secure-energy-transitions/executive-summary); [PDF](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/cb39c1bf-d2b3-446d-8c35-aae6b1f3a4a0/BatteriesandSecureEnergyTransitions.pdf)

- Lithium-ion **battery prices** (the pack/cell series, Figure 1.2, cited to BNEF): from about **$800/kWh** down to **less than $140/kWh in 2023**. LFP cell prices were **below $100/kWh in March 2024** (Benchmark Mineral Intelligence). In 2023, prices were lowest in **China** and about **10–20% higher in the United States and Europe**.
- No absolute 2024 or 2030 **$/kWh for a 4-hour installed system** was recoverable from the text. The executive summary does say that, even in the Stated Policies Scenario, **total upfront costs of utility-scale battery storage projects — battery plus installation, other components, and developer costs — decline by 40% by 2030**.
- Average duration of the global battery fleet: about **2 hours today**, **2.7 hours in 2030**, **3.4 hours in 2050** (STEPS). Four-hour systems are the US capacity-market norm; the fleet is not yet a 4-hour fleet.

**JRC, system vs pack, 2024 BNEF converted to euros** — JRC145289, *Battery Technology in the European Union* (text read from [this PDF copy](https://3e-news.net/web/files/articles/69168/pdf_file/JRC145289_01.pdf))

- BNEF 2024 survey, JRC conversion at the **2024 average rate 1 USD = 0.9243 EUR**, described as **real 2024 euros** for the pack series: global volume-weighted average lithium-ion price **106 EUR/kWh** (−20% real). BEV packs **90 EUR/kWh**.
- **BESS at system level: 153 EUR/kWh, excluding EPC and grid connection**, −40% year-on-year. Footnote: global volume-weighted average **across all durations**. At the same FX, 153 / 0.9243 ≈ **$166/kWh** (derived), in line with BNEF’s unrevised 2024 turnkey headline of $165/kWh.
- China cells **50–55 EUR/kWh**. China **packs $94/kWh** (−25% y/y). **US packs 31% higher, Europe packs 48% higher** than China (derived: about **$123/kWh US** and **$139/kWh Europe**). These are packs.

**S&P Global via JRC, Germany delivery price** — JRC141463, [PDF](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC141463/JRC141463_01.pdf), Figure 35, source line “S&P Global Commodity Insights, ©2025”

- CAPEX for lithium-ion BESS **fell from over $300/kWh in 2022 to below $200/kWh for 2025 delivery in Germany**. Installation and development costs “remain region-dependent.” The figure note says **“DC: direct current,”** so this Germany number may be a **DC system price**, not a fully installed AC project. LFP CAPEX declined **37% from 2022 to 2025** (same section, citation [24] in that report).

**ENTSO-E / ENTSOG TYNDP 2024, European planning cost, 4-hour utility** — [Scenarios Methodology Report, final, January 2025](https://2024.entsos-tyndp-scenarios.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/TYNDP_2024_Scenarios_Methodology_Report_Final_Version_250128.pdf), Annex II

  - **2040 €627,122/MW** (derived **€157/kWh**)

- **Residential battery, 5 kW / 12.5 kWh** (2.5 h): 2030 CAPEX **€1,731,283/MW** (derived about **€693/kWh**).
- Generation CAPEX in TYNDP is mostly the Danish Energy Agency catalogue; **offshore and batteries use other sources**, named as the Annual Technology Baseline and hydrogen-hub cost studies. Scenario bands on technology costs are only **±1% in 2030** (±15% in 2040, ±20% in 2050). There is **no 2025 column**.

**DEA-method catalogue for Colombia (fixed 2024 USD, explicit 4-hour, small unit)** — [Technology catalogue hosted by the Danish Energy Agency](https://ens.dk/media/8617/download), chapter 14, datasheet 14.13.1. Not a Danish domestic cost. Fixed **2024 USD**, excluding VAT. Reference unit: **1 MW / 4 MWh**.

- Total investment: **2024: 0.47 million USD/MWh** (uncertainty **0.35–0.54**), also stated as **1.89 million USD/MW** (uncertainty **1.40–2.14**). That is **$470/kWh** or **$1,890/kW**.
- 2024 split: energy **0.35 million USD/MWh**, power **0.33 million USD/MW**, other project costs **0.04 million USD/MWh**. Check: 0.35×4 + 0.33 + 0.04×4 = 1.89 million USD per MW.
- Fixed O&M **$15,000/MW-year** in 2024 (uncertainty **$5,000–50,000**). Variable O&M **$2.00/MWh**.
- This 2024 central case is far above BNEF, Ember, and IRENA 2024–2025 market surveys. The reference plant is **1 MW**, and the bibliography still cites older pack-price commentary (including a BNEF “$139/kWh” item). Treat it as a **high, small-unit, catalogue** bound, not as the 2025 European market clearing price.

**Wood Mackenzie**

- *Europe utility-scale energy storage pricing report 2025* (1 Sep 2025) is public only as a paid abstract: LFP, utility-scale Europe, **real 2025 euros**, 10-year forecast by system and component. **No $/kWh or €/kWh is in the public summary.** — [Wood Mackenzie](https://www.woodmac.com/reports/power-markets-europe-utility-scale-energy-storage-pricing-report-2025-150402381/)

### Inferences
- A like-for-like reading for **Europe 2025** is: stationary **packs well below $100/kWh globally** ($70/kWh BNEF), **Europe turnkey systems about $177/kWh** (BNEF, likely ex-EPC and ex-grid), and **all-in project costs for non-China, non-US markets about $125/kWh** (Ember, 4 h+, pulled down by India and Saudi Arabia as well as Italy). Germany “2025 delivery below $200/kWh” (S&P via JRC) sits between the Europe turnkey number and a fully soft-costed US install.
- The model’s Germany/UK **$253/kWh ($1,011/kW) in 2025** is **above** BNEF’s 2025 Europe turnkey price and Ember’s ex-China/ex-US all-in average, and **below** NREL’s US overnight cost and Lazard’s US high case. It is in the same neighborhood as **2024** Europe turnkey (~$275/kWh) plus a modest install adder, or as 2025 Europe turnkey plus a high grid-connection adder.
- IRENA’s **$192/kWh (2024)** and **$273/kWh (2023)** are global installed averages dominated by China and the US. They are weak as a Germany/UK point estimate and useful as a world midpoint.

## What is the published low/central/high spread, and how much is cell or pack versus installed AC system?

### Takeaway
The spread is large even inside one year: roughly **$70/kWh** (global stationary pack, or China turnkey) to about **$300–400/kWh** (US fully installed high case). For a 4-hour system, the **pack is on the order of one-third to one-half of a Europe or global turnkey system price**, and **installation plus grid connection is on the order of $50/kWh**, with connection alone spanning **$30–100/kWh**. US soft costs (EPC, tax, development, studies) push overnight cost well above the battery cabinet.

### Cited Findings

- **Pack vs turnkey, 2025, global:** stationary pack **$70/kWh** vs 4-hour turnkey **$110/kWh** vs all-duration turnkey **$117/kWh** (BNEF, via the sources above). Pack is about **64%** of the global 4-hour turnkey price (70/110, derived) and is **not** the installed project.
- **Regional turnkey, 2025:** China **$73/kWh**, global **$117/kWh**, Europe **$177/kWh**, US **$219/kWh**. Europe/China ≈ **2.4×**. US/China ≈ **3.0×**.
- **2024 packs by region** (JRC on BNEF): China **$94/kWh**, Europe about **48% higher**, US about **31% higher**.
- **Ember all-in, Oct 2025, ex-China ex-US, 4 h+:** equipment **~$75/kWh** (60%) and installation plus connection **~$50/kWh** (40%). Connection fees **$30–100/kWh**. Local-content or tariff cases can add **≥$100/kWh** on equipment.
- **NREL 2024 US 4-hour overnight $334/kWh (2024$):** cabinets **$210/kWh** (~63%). The rest is inverter, electrical and structural BOS (including transformer and substation equipment), labor, permitting and interconnection studies, tax, contingency, overhead, and profit. Energy-related **$241/kWh** vs power-related **$372/kW**. Interconnection **upgrades** are outside this total.
- **Lazard US 4-hour, June 2025:** DC block **$107–232/kWh**; EPC **$28–116/kWh**; AC power electronics **$25–67/kW**. The DC block is the largest piece, but EPC at the high end is comparable to a large share of the DC block. Full installed range **$155–400/kWh** (derived from $62–160 million).
- **IRENA 2023:** 4-hour turnkey about **20%** below 1-hour turnkey, on a global range of **$260–325/kWh** that still **excludes** EPC, grid connection, and development. So duration and “fully installed” are separate adjustments. Applying both can move a 1-hour equipment price by well over 20%.
- **Colombia/DEA-method 4-hour, 1 MW, 2024 USD:** total **$470/kWh** (range **$350–540/kWh**). Energy component **$350/kWh**, power **$330/kW** (**$82.5/kWh** at 4 h), other **$40/kWh**. Energy is about **three-quarters** of this high catalogue total.
- **JRC145289 manufacturing cost stack** (cell production, not an installed BESS): labour **$2–25/kWh** by location, energy **$1–7/kWh**, land and building **$2–7/kWh**, materials **$64–73/kWh**. Useful only to show that **materials dominate the cell**, not the project.

### Inferences
- For a Europe sensitivity around the model’s country costs, the **low tail is China turnkey (~$73/kWh) or Ember all-in ex-China/ex-US ($125/kWh)**. The **high tail is a US fully installed plant ($334/kWh NREL central 2024, up to ~$400/kWh Lazard)**. Europe 2025 turnkey **$177/kWh** is the best public **central equipment** anchor, not the fully installed anchor.
- Adding Ember’s **~$50/kWh** install-and-connection to BNEF Europe turnkey **$177/kWh** gives about **$227/kWh** (derived). That is still **below** the model’s **$253/kWh**. Using the top of Ember’s connection range (**$100/kWh**) on the Europe turnkey price gives about **$277/kWh** before any extra European EPC, which **brackets** the model.
- Behind-the-meter use does not remove the battery cabinet, inverter, or transformer. It can avoid **deep** grid reinforcement and some merchant-plant development cost. NREL already excludes interconnection upgrade costs. Ember’s $30–100/kWh connection item is the main block that a site with spare import capacity might not pay in full.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2025 Europe source was found that publishes a single low/central/high for **fully installed 4-hour AC** CAPEX with EPC and grid connection itemised in euros.
- The BNEF 2025 “turnkey” boundary (DC block vs AC vs EPC) is inferred from IRENA’s 2023 description of BNEF turnkey, not confirmed on the 2025 survey page.

## What 2030 forecasts exist, and do they assume learning rather than CPI?

### Takeaway
Every 2030 forecast retrieved here is a **real-cost path in which battery costs fall, or at most stabilise, because of learning, scale, and chemistry**. None of them inflate today’s CAPEX with CPI. The pace disagrees: BNEF still has steep declines out to **2035**, IEA has **−40% by 2030** on full project upfront cost from a 2023/24 baseline, NREL’s US mid case falls only to **$279/kWh by 2030 (2024$)**, and S&P’s Germany narrative is **decline through 2025 then stabilisation by 2030**.

### Cited Findings

- **IEA STEPS (2024 report):** utility-scale project upfront cost (battery + installation + other components + developer costs) **−40% by 2030**. Separately, global average lithium-ion **battery** costs **−40% from 2023 to 2030** from further chemistry and manufacturing innovation, in the net-zero discussion. Both statements are technology learning, not inflation. Absolute 2030 $/kWh was not in the retrieved text. — [IEA PDF](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/cb39c1bf-d2b3-446d-8c35-aae6b1f3a4a0/BatteriesandSecureEnergyTransitions.pdf)
- **BNEF system survey, forecast year is 2035 not 2030** (Energy-Storage.News, 16 Dec 2025): average **4-hour turnkey** prices **China $41/kWh**, **Europe $101/kWh**, **US systems using Chinese batteries $108/kWh**. Drivers named: larger cells (≥300 Ah DC systems **50%** cheaper than smaller cells), ≥4 MWh DC blocks **39%** cheaper than 2–4 MWh blocks, integration, and manufacturing efficiency. History in that article is **inflation-adjusted** (2024 restated from $165 to $169/kWh), so the outlook is a real decline, not a nominal CPI path. **No 2030 point** was in the article. US tariffs and foreign-entity-of-concern tax-credit rules are noted as a US wedge, not a European one.
- **NREL 2025 update, 4-hour, 2024$/kWh, US overnight fully installed:** **2030 low/mid/high = $207 / $279 / $354**. **2035: $147 / $243 / $339**. Low and mid are the min and median of published projections at 2026, 2035, and 2050, interpolated; high is the max. The **high case rises** after 2024 (2026 peak **$366/kWh**) because some publications include supply-chain constraints and **tariffs**, and it does not return to the 2024 level until the late 2030s. CPI is used only to put source studies into **2024 dollars**. The paths themselves are real learning curves. — [Cole et al. 2025 PDF](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy25osti/93281.pdf)
- **TYNDP 2024 Annex II, real terms, utility 4-hour:** **€716,480/MW in 2030**, then **€627,122/MW in 2040** and **€537,562/MW in 2050**. Decline is built in. Price year not stated. — [TYNDP methodology](https://2024.entsos-tyndp-scenarios.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/TYNDP_2024_Scenarios_Methodology_Report_Final_Version_250128.pdf)
- **S&P via JRC141463:** Germany BESS CAPEX down to **below $200/kWh for 2025 delivery**, with **stabilisation expected by 2030**. That is a learning path that **flattens**, not a CPI path and not BNEF’s continued drop to ~$100/kWh by 2035. C&I **2-hour** system cost in Figure 38 is described as **−30% by 2030 versus 2022**; the chart’s absolute $/kWh was not extracted.
- **Colombia/DEA-method catalogue, fixed 2024 USD, 1 MW, 4-hour:** total investment **0.28 million USD/MWh in 2030** (**$280/kWh**, **$1,140/kW**) and **0.20 million USD/MWh in 2050** (uncertainty **0.19–0.22**). Fixed O&M **$10,830/MW-year in 2030**. The projection method is the catalogue’s learning-rate appendix, in constant 2024 dollars. The 2030 level is high relative to BNEF’s 2035 Europe turnkey and to TYNDP 2030.
- **Danish AF25 storage note:** battery **costs** are taken from the latest technology catalogue; the note itself is a **capacity** projection (pipeline to 2030), not a new CAPEX forecast. — [AF25 ellagring](https://ens.dk/media/7604/download)

### Inferences
- The model’s nominal path from **$1,011/kW (2025)** to **$727/kW (2030)** is a **decline of about 28% in five years**. That is **in the same direction as every learning forecast** and **slower than** “another 40% off an already-fallen 2025 price.” IEA’s −40% runs from a **2023/early-2024** baseline. A large part of that decline is already in the 2023–2025 data (BNEF turnkey −40% in 2024 and −31% in 2025). Applying a further 40% to 2025 Europe prices would double-count.
- Holding the 2025 Europe cost flat in real terms to 2030 matches the **S&P stabilisation** story and is **higher** than BNEF’s 2035 Europe turnkey of **$101/kWh** and higher than TYNDP’s **€179/kWh** only if today’s fully installed Europe cost is already near €180/kWh. If the model’s $253/kWh is the 2025 installed cost, TYNDP’s €179/kWh in 2030 (about **$194/kWh** at JRC’s 2024 rate of 0.9243 EUR per USD; derived, and the TYNDP price year may differ) is a **moderate real decline**, close to the model’s **$182/kWh**.
- A CPI-only sensitivity (2025 CAPEX inflated to 2030) is **not** what these sources do. It would move the opposite direction from BNEF, IEA, NREL mid/low, TYNDP, and S&P.

### Gaps
- No public BNEF **2030** Europe 4-hour system price was found (the cited forecast year is **2035**).
- IEA’s 40% figure is not accompanied, in the retrieved text, by the 2023 $/kWh starting point for the full project.
- TYNDP price year is missing, so the euro figures cannot be converted to 2025 or 2030 USD without an extra assumption.

## What low, central, and high 4-hour CAPEX is defensible for an EU industrial site using the battery only as storage behind a heat pump?

### Takeaway
Use **utility-scale 4-hour system costs**, not residential and not the 1 MW / 2-hour C&I Lazard case, if the industrial battery is multi-megawatt. Behind-the-meter operation can sit a bit **below** a merchant plant that pays deep grid reinforcement, but it should not be priced at China turnkey or at a stationary pack. A defensible **2025** band for a **fully installed** Europe 4-hour system is about **$125–177/kWh low, about $200–250/kWh central, and about $275–400/kWh high** (see anchors below). For **2030 in real dollars**, a central case near the model’s **$182/kWh** is supported by TYNDP; a low case near **$100–140/kWh** requires continued learning of the BNEF/IEA type; a high case near **$280/kWh** is the NREL US mid path or a stalled Europe installed cost.

### Cited Findings

Behind-the-meter versus utility, from the same sources:

- **IEA:** in 2023, about **65%** of battery power additions were utility-scale and **35%** behind-the-meter. In China, many behind-the-meter systems are **large commercial and industrial** installations “close to utility-scale systems in terms of size and cost.” Residential is a different market. — [IEA PDF](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/cb39c1bf-d2b3-446d-8c35-aae6b1f3a4a0/BatteriesandSecureEnergyTransitions.pdf)
- **Lazard June 2025:** 1 MW, 2-hour C&I DC capital **$238–445/kWh** versus 100 MW, 4-hour utility DC **$107–232/kWh**. Small C&I is much more expensive per kWh. The comparison is not duration-matched.
- **IRENA:** Germany residential **$862/kWh in Q3 2023** versus utility **project** cost **$273/kWh in 2023** globally.
- **TYNDP 2030:** residential **~€693/kWh** (derived) versus utility 4-hour **€179/kWh** (derived).
- **JRC141463:** falling system costs improve the **C&I** case (peak shaving, demand charges, avoided grid upgrades). The only numeric C&I statement extracted is **−30% by 2030 vs 2022** for a **2-hour** C&I system, without a 2025 $/kWh level.
- A heat-pump site that does not sell into capacity or ancillary markets still buys the same **energy storage hardware**. What changes is mostly **grid-connection depth, land, and developer margin**, which Ember puts in the **~$50/kWh** install-and-connection bucket (range **$30–100/kWh** on connection alone) and which NREL largely **excludes** when the upgrade is a network reinforcement.

### Inferences

Suggested sensitivity for an **EU industrial, multi-MW, ~4-hour** lithium-ion battery used only as electric storage behind a heat pump. Figures are **real 2025 USD** for the first row and **real ~2030 USD** for the second (learning, not CPI). $/kW = $/kWh × 4, matching the model’s storage convention. These are judgment bands placed on the anchors above, not a new survey.

- **2025 low: $150/kWh ($600/kW).** Above Ember’s $125/kWh ex-China/ex-US all-in (that average includes lower-cost non-European auctions) and below BNEF Europe turnkey $177/kWh. Appropriate if the site uses a simple AC-coupled block, imports standard LFP containers, and has **spare connection capacity** so Ember’s connection adder is at its low end. Not appropriate if the site must build a new substation.
- **2025 central: $230/kWh ($920/kW).** BNEF Europe 2025 turnkey plus about $50/kWh install and connection. The model’s **$253/kWh ($1,011/kW)** is a reasonable **central-to-slightly-high** point inside this band, not an outlier.
- **2025 high: $330/kWh ($1,320/kW).** Near NREL’s US overnight $334/kWh (2024$), below Lazard’s $400/kWh high and well below the $470/kWh small-unit catalogue. Use if European EPC, standards, and grid works resemble a US fully soft-costed plant, or if 2025 Europe prices partially reverse.
- **2030 low: $120/kWh ($480/kW).** Near BNEF’s **2035** Europe 4-hour turnkey ($101/kWh) plus a reduced install adder. This assumes learning continues after 2025. It is aggressive relative to S&P’s “stabilisation by 2030.”
- **2030 central: $180/kWh ($720/kW).** Matches the model (**$182/kWh, $727/kW**) and TYNDP’s **€179/kWh**. This is a real decline of roughly 20–30% from a 2025 installed cost around $230–250/kWh, consistent with partial further learning rather than with freezing 2025 prices or with CPI uplift.
- **2030 high: $280/kWh ($1,120/kW).** NREL 2030 **mid** case in 2024$ ($279/kWh), also the Colombia catalogue’s 2030 central case. Use if US-like soft costs persist and Europe does not keep the 2024–2025 price crash. NREL’s 2030 **high** ($354/kWh) is a tariff/supply-chain stress case for the US, not the default EU high.

Do **not** put China **$73/kWh (2025 turnkey)** or **$41/kWh (2035)** into the EU low case except as a labeled floor on **equipment** if the project can actually procure at Chinese domestic system prices and avoid European install and compliance costs. IEA’s 2023 observation that Europe and US **pack** prices were only 10–20% above China is **out of date** relative to JRC/BNEF 2024 (Europe packs ~48% above China) and to 2025 **system** prices (Europe turnkey $177 vs China $73).

If the battery is closer to **1 MW** than to tens of MW, shift toward Lazard’s C&I premium (DC capital already **$238–445/kWh** on a 2-hour unit) and the Colombia catalogue’s **1 MW** total (**$470/kWh in 2024**). Residential TYNDP and German residential IRENA prices (**about €700/kWh and $860/kWh**) are the wrong analogue for an industrial heat-pump battery.

### Gaps
- No source states a CAPEX specifically for a battery that only buffers a heat pump. The hardware cost is taken from utility and C&I storage; the application changes dispatch and revenue, not the container price, except through size, duration, and interconnection scope.
- The recommended $150 / $230 / $330 and $120 / $180 / $280 figures are **inferences** placed on published anchors so they can be used as a sensitivity. They are not themselves published “low/central/high” scenarios from one institution.
- Nominal-versus-real: the model baseline is described as nominal USD. The public 2030 paths above are real declines. A nominal 2030 price slightly above these real numbers would be the CPI wedge only. That wedge was not calculated here.
